Cisco·2012–2018

Running a $54M Services business

Six years owning the Services P&L and every engagement for four strategic enterprise accounts, then founding a new practice that turned a delivery organization into a source of growth.

The challenge

Cisco's largest enterprise customers in the Midwest bought services from many parts of the company, and nobody owned the whole relationship. Delivery was strong engagement by engagement and inconsistent account by account.

The services business was also measured as a cost of selling hardware. To matter, it had to show it could create its own pipeline.

What I led

  • Owned a combined Services P&L of $54M and all Services delivery for State Farm, Allstate, Caterpillar, and CNA Insurance as the named Services Executive.
  • Led 100+ matrixed delivery professionals, with 20 to 80 Cisco, partner, and offshore resources in flight at any time.
  • Built C-level relationships at each account and made delivery performance a standing topic in the executive conversation.
  • Founded the Lifecycle Management Practice, a new offer that followed customers through adoption and renewal rather than ending at go-live.
  • Created the governance for a large-scale PMO at a world-renowned healthcare organization and led the team delivering it.

What changed

Each strategic account had one services executive who could speak for every engagement, forecast accurately, and escalate early. Retention and expansion followed from that.

The new practice gave the organization an offer customers asked for by name, and $5M of pipeline that did not exist before.

The result

$28.8M in new Services revenue delivered, $5M in new pipeline from the Lifecycle Management Practice, and long-term retention across four of Cisco's most strategic enterprise accounts.
